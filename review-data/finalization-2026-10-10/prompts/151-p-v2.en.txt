PAINTING MEDIUM AND BOOK STYLE
Paint a genuine, moderately textured hand-painted watercolor on white watercolor paper. Use luminous overlapping translucent washes, broad varied strokes and gentle pigment pooling at a few natural edges. This layered watercolor must visibly exist INSIDE the characters' heads, torsos, arms, legs and hands, as well as in the mural, masonry and paving. Leave some lighter paper showing through the paint. Avoid smooth flat fills, vector shapes, marker shading, colored-pencil grain, plastic gloss, airbrushed 3D surfaces and exaggerated stains. Define the green character with restrained darker green contours and the coral character with darker coral or burgundy contours, never black outlines around their bodies. Faces use small dark facial marks. Ground the people with quiet watercolor contact shadows under their feet. The outer painted edges are organic and irregular, with clean white paper around all four sides. Keep every body, both feet, brush, bucket, sign and the entire tripod within its own scene with breathing room. The top and middle must be fully framed, rather than showing cropped bodies above a gutter.

CHARACTER DESIGN
These are stylized adult book characters with medium, balanced, neutral bodies. Their torsos are simple and almost straight sided; their arms are modest in width, their legs have believable adult length, and their rounded feet are planted naturally. They are neither skinny nor swollen, and they are not babies, chibi figures or toys. Every head is a true CIRCLE of equal width and height, with no oval extension, pointed chin, jaw, ears, nose, hair or separate neck. The head joins the shoulders directly; show a soft darker local-color crescent contact shadow beneath the round head so its boundary is visible. Gender never changes the silhouette: no breasts, pinched waist, accentuated hips or buttocks, bulges or indentations. Green can have a few fine SHORT upper-outer eyelashes attached to the SAME small dark oval eyes as the other character. Do not enlarge the eyes or turn them into a different eye style. Tiny white glints may occur INSIDE the dark eyes; there is no surrounding white sclera. Closed happy arc eyes are appropriate for coral in the last scene. Use small expressive eyebrows and a friendly curved mouth, occasionally a small open happy mouth. No sharp pink blush circles; any cheek warmth is an extremely soft wash blended into the local body color. Each hand has THREE fingers and ONE thumb; a brush or bucket handle can naturally obscure some digits. Use believable grips and connected limbs, not floating hands. Both wear the SAME plain, straight, knee-length cream artist apron, with simple shoulder straps, one small front pocket and restrained green, blue and warm paint marks. No other clothing, accessories or gendered styling.
