## What the book is doing

The book is a warm, witty visual interpretation of Kevin Kelly's short advice about learning, creativity, kindness, responsibility, habits, relationships, work, money, and living thoughtfully. The Persian edition title is «کاش زودتر می‌دونستم». These illustrations are small moral stories or visual discoveries. An image often turns an abstract relationship into a physical action: generosity grows fruit, outrage becomes a cloud, an unsolicited call becomes a hand reaching through a phone, or focused attention becomes a concentrated sunbeam. The best images offer a second layer beyond the words.

The desired emotional range includes comic surprise, tender recognition, curiosity, relief, anxiety, and quiet reflection. The book is family-friendly, but does not erase difficult subjects: grief, manipulation, fear, anger, and bad decisions appear as gentle symbolic scenes. Avoid cruel caricature or humiliating the character whose mistake teaches the lesson.

## Character design

- The ordinary cast consists of friendly pastel humanoids with very round bald heads, a simple rounded body, short rounded feet, rounded hands, and smooth, featureless anatomy. They have no realistic skin, nose, ears, hair, or anatomical nudity details. Maintain the proportions of the reference characters; do not drift into photorealistic people, stick figures, generic emoji heads, or glossy 3D toys.
- Express emotion through black oval eyes, lifted or lowered eyebrows, small expressive mouths, tiny cheek blush, head tilt, shoulders, and hand gestures. Some eyes contain a tiny white highlight. The face stays readable at book size.
- Female gender is conveyed by eyelashes, as the user explicitly states and examples 3, 10, 28, 98, 110, 131, and 147 confirm. Eyelashes remain visible even behind glasses. Male or unspecified characters generally have simpler eyes without lashes. Do not use body shape, pink color, hair, or makeup as a substitute.
- Color is not a fixed identity or morality system. Blue may be thoughtful, anxious, creative, amused, or calm; green may be kind, irritated, focused, or curious; purple may be a woman, a child, a boss, an elder, or a regretful character. Choose a balanced pastel palette and then keep an individual character's color, lashes, glasses, costume, and scale consistent through its story.
- Glasses, a moustache, walking stick, hat, apron, laboratory coat, or chef uniform sometimes carry age or professional role. Use these when they help the scene. Ordinary characters frequently have no costume; their smooth abstract bodies remain family-friendly.
- Children use the same character language at smaller scale with a slightly larger head in proportion to the body. Older people may use glasses, a stick, softened posture, or moustache. A few author portraits use a more human face and grey hair; that is an exception, not the default character model.

## Painting and scene language

- Watercolor on visibly textured paper, with transparent washes, uneven pigment pooling, soft mottled shadows, and hand-drawn colored-pencil or ink contours. Late examples 131 and 133 use crisp darker contours but retain painted interiors and organic texture.
- Soft, irregular painted edges fade into clean white paper. Do not add heavy rectangular frames, thick comic borders, glossy gradients, vector-perfect outlines, or full-bleed photographic realism.
- Pastel colors can be vivid at focal points. Warm honey yellow, apricot, mint/lime green, sky blue/turquoise, lavender, rose pink, and wood browns recur. Dark indigo night or storm imagery is used for meaningful mood contrast. Color changes should not arbitrarily change a character's identity between panels.
- Settings feel inhabited: wooden furniture and floors, patterned rugs, books, plants, pots, windows, stone walls, shelves, warm daylight, little flowers, or gentle city architecture. These details support a memorable place rather than crowding the narrative. Focus props must remain easy to recognize.
- Painterly backgrounds are richer than the minimal character anatomy. Characters and important props have the clearest contour and highest local contrast; distant scenery is softer.
- Light and color can deepen emotion: #7 and #35 contrast storm clouds with warm inner calm; #12 uses campfire warmth inside an indigo forest; #48 uses uncertain colored silhouettes emerging from a misty background. Other scenes use colors simply for beauty and contrast.

## Persian dialogue and reading order

- All new speech and thought bubbles should be in Persian, honoring the user's explicit requirement. Use short natural colloquial Persian, matching the translation's approachable voice. Do not paste the whole advice into a bubble.
- Speech bubbles use a pointed tail; thoughts use rounded/scalloped cloud forms with small trailing circles. Associate the tail or thought trail clearly with the correct character. Bubbles have pale/white interiors, a thin colored or dark outline, comfortable padding, and bold readable lettering.
- Exact wording, spelling, joining, punctuation, and right-to-left direction must be inspected after generation. A bubble that only resembles Persian is a failure. Preserve meaningful zero-width non-joiners where the wording needs them.
- Within a sequential horizontal strip, use right-to-left progression. #133 is explicitly numbered ۱ on the right, ۲ in the middle, and ۳ on the left. Vertical sequences generally read top-to-bottom. Comparison diptychs can have a labeled left/right contrast, but the distinction must be clear.
- Some older prop labels contain Latin words such as CREDIT CARD or ERROR. Those are source exceptions, not authorization to put English in new dialogue. Prefer Persian for meaningful signs or labels unless the user chooses otherwise.

## Storytelling mechanisms observed

1. **A consequence returns as a physical gift — #133.** At a bakery, green praises a baker to yellow while blue works with his back turned. Yellow passes along the praise to pink and purple; blue overhears with delight. In the final panel, a heart-shaped loaf is given to green. The warm bakery, heart motif, and expressions turn “compliment people behind their back” into a social story. It uses three RTL panels with persistent characters and a visual payoff rather than a generic person saying a compliment.
2. **The same material becomes a different story — #117.** Top panel: a green character cowers before an imposing balloon monster and prepares to attack it with a pin. Bottom: the balloons have been thoughtfully reshaped into a friendly dog that green gently pets. The repeated setting and material make imagination visible. This is cleverer than merely showing “fearful” and “brave.”
3. **A physical experiment explains an abstract principle — #136.** Top: a blue character has multiple magnifying glasses scattering available light. Bottom: one lens concentrates the same sun into a beam that cuts a rope. Same place, sunlight, and character; a clear change of method produces the result. No paragraph of labels is required.
4. **An accident becomes value — #80.** A broken mirror sits behind green making its pieces into a beautiful mosaic, while a small yellow child holds the ball that caused the break. This adds responsibility, ingenuity, and a tender relational detail to “do not waste a crisis.”
5. **Role changes reveal the lesson — #51.** The green expert critiques the little blue learner with a magnifier and red crosses; the same pair then draw together, with a shared pencil activity and a lightbulb. The emotional change from worried recipient to happy collaborator carries the advice about asking for guidance.
6. **Visual irony — #87.** A sculptor reveals a statue with its hands covering its mouth in astonishment. The joke is embedded in the artwork itself, not in a separate caption. Stone chips and the unfinished base explain the making process. Its literal “leaving out” metaphor is playful while the craft details keep it concrete.
7. **A little future inside the present — #98.** The same purple character's childhood volcano project and adult laboratory success are tied together with a dashed arrow. The scientific setting and eyelashes/glasses persist, connecting traits rather than merely comparing age.
8. **An object foreshadows a later scene — #142 and #113.** #142-e is a phone with a hand reaching toward a safe labeled «اطلاعات حساب»; #142 expands the same objects into a room and a blue character who takes control, saying «خودم وارد می‌شوم.» #113-e turns an urgent wristwatch into a tyrant with moustache and an angry face; #113 expands into a workplace conflict where another character quietly prioritizes the important work.

## Composition and book fit

Single scenes may be square, portrait, or landscape. Multi-panel stories include two stacked landscape panels in a portrait whole (#117, #143), two side-by-side comparison panels (#51, #137), three narrow horizontal story panels (#133), or a 2×2 grid (#89). Use the panel count demanded by the idea, not a mandatory template.

For long bilingual advice that spans pages, the small companion is a focused vignette featuring one key object or an intriguing detail; the main illustration completes the concept with action and emotion. The companion and main share props, palette, and visual logic. Do not produce a shrunk duplicate of the full illustration. In the PDF, #23, #113, and #142 are explicit two-page examples.

Most finished pages place English at the top, an image in the middle, Persian translation below, and sometimes a brief translator explanation marked with a small sprout/check. The raw generated image should carry only useful in-scene text, not these full outside-page sentences. Source layouts sometimes move translation before the image or use a landscape strip, so format should stay adaptable.

## Prompt construction and quality gate

Each candidate should have a distinct narrative mechanism, not merely a different camera angle. State the sentence's deeper insight in one private planning sentence, invent a concrete situation, identify the character's desire and action, and specify the surprising consequence or emotional discovery. Then specify the composition, character continuity, palette, exact Persian bubble text and ownership, important props and their placement, painting material, and what must remain legible at print size.

For #151, the work must visibly change the person: patience, skill, generosity, confidence, or inner capacity should grow. A money pile versus a happy face is too shallow; a trophy may accidentally make “become” look like merely acquiring status. Do not imply that paid work or ownership is shameful. The insight is about what guides the work. A craft, garden, music practice, or helping relationship can make growth tangible; show a little unexpected story rather than an abstract motivational poster.

Before accepting a candidate, inspect: does it explain the advice without the sentence; is the wit kind; is there one readable focal story; do figures match the reference anatomy; are eyelashes and character colors consistent; is every Persian bubble correct; do panels have an obvious reading order; do props behave coherently; and do texture, contours, and white paper edges belong to the same book? Save the user's selected image and feedback separately from rejected candidates, with the exact generation prompt, source references, revision history, and resulting style preference. Treat the present study as the initial hypothesis to be refined by the user's selections.
